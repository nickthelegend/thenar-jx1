**Implied actuator market (ESTIMATED).** Method: units × BOM or ASP × actuator share. "Actuators" means body joints only unless noted.

| Scenario | Arithmetic | Actuator market |
|---|---|---|
| 2025 actual | 13k units × US$35k BOM × 51% | **≈US$0.23 bn** (plus non-humanoid sales by joint makers such as RobStride and Encos) |
| 2030 BofA | 1.2 M × US$17k × 51% | **≈US$10.4 bn**; with dexterous hands (70%) ≈US$14.3 bn |
| 2030 MS global | US$28 bn OEM revenue × 30–35% (actuators as a share of *price*, assuming BOM ≈60–70% of price) | **≈US$8.4–9.8 bn** |
| 2030 GS | 0.89 M × assumed ASP US$25–35k = US$22–31 bn × 30–35% | **≈US$6.7–10.9 bn** |
| 2035 GS | US$138 bn × 30–35% | **≈US$41–48 bn** |
| 2035 BofA | 10 M × US$13–17k × 51% | **≈US$66–87 bn** (≈US$91 bn with hands at the US$13k BOM) |
| 2035 UBS | US$30–50 bn TAM × 25–30% | **≈US$7.5–15 bn** |

- **Central reading:** about US$8–14 bn of humanoid actuators in 2030, and US$10–90 bn in 2035.
- **Price deflation:** the per-joint price must fall about 70%, from about US$1,000 to about US$250–320 per joint at 30 joints (xenospectrum, UNVERIFIED). An Indian entrant will compete on price against Chinese suppliers whose costs are already near that level.

**Scale check (ESTIMATED):** the industrial-robot market is worth about US$18–19 bn a year in robot value. A 6-axis robot has 6 servo motors and reducers, so 603k robots means about 3.6 M servo-actuator sets a year. In 2030, BofA's 1.2 M humanoids × 30 joints ≈ **36 M joint actuators a year**, about 10× the current industrial-robot joint count. These are smaller, lower-precision and far cheaper per unit.

### 3.3 Copper per humanoid: bottom-up estimate (ESTIMATED; method shown)

Reference design: BofA's 30-joint body (16 rotary + 14 linear) plus 2 dexterous hands with 6–12 micro-motors each. Battery 0.9–2.5 kWh (G1 ≈0.9 kWh; Optimus ≈2.3 kWh).

**Copper per motor class.** Rule of thumb: copper is about 25–35% of a frameless PMSM stator's mass, and end turns add 30–60% for short stacks.

| Component | Low | High | Method |
|---|---|---|---|
| Large leg/hip motors (≈100–120 mm stator) | 8 × 0.15 kg = 1.20 | 12 × 0.35 kg = 4.20 | e.g. a 100 mm OD × 25 mm stator weighs ≈0.6–0.8 kg, of which ≈0.15–0.35 kg is copper |
| Medium arm/torso motors (≈60–80 mm) | 10 × 0.06 = 0.60 | 12 × 0.15 = 1.80 | scaled by stator volume |
| Small wrist/neck motors (≈40 mm) | 6 × 0.02 = 0.12 | 10 × 0.05 = 0.50 | |
| Hand micro-motors (coreless, 10–16 mm) | 12 × 0.003 = 0.04 | 24 × 0.01 = 0.24 | |
| **Motor windings subtotal** | **≈2.0 kg** | **≈6.7 kg** | |
| Harness (power bus + CAN/EtherCAT) | 0.3 | 1.0 | 10–25 m of 1.3–3.3 mm² power cable (12–30 g Cu/m) + signal |
| Joint drivers + main PCBs | 0.2 | 0.6 | 30–40 driver boards at 5–15 g Cu (heavy-copper boards) |
| Battery (current collectors, busbars) | 1.0 | 3.0 | 0.9–2.5 kWh × ≈1.1–1.2 kg/kWh (ICA/IDTechEx) |
| Connectors, busbars, Cu heat pipes, BMS | 0.1 | 0.5 | |
| **Total per humanoid** | **≈3.6 kg** | **≈11.8 kg** | **Central ≈6–8 kg** |

**The bottom-up estimate is consistent with the published range.**

**Copper cost per robot (ESTIMATED):**

- **Copper in the robot:** 8 kg × US$14.4/kg (LME, Oct 2026) ≈ **US$115**.
- **As magnet wire:** ≈US$16.5/kg for the motor share.
- **Share of BOM:** under 1% of a US$17k BOM.
- **Sensitivity:** a US$5,000/t copper rise adds about US$40 per robot.
- **Conclusion:** copper price is **not** a material driver of actuator cost. Magnets (NdFeB, 1–4 kg), reducers and screws matter far more.

## 6. Key uncertainties

1. **Forecast inflation.** GS raised its 2035 forecast 4.6× in one revision, and MS raised China 2026 3.6× within one year. Bank forecasts follow equity sentiment.
2. **Primary access.** GS, MS, UBS, Citi and JPM notes are paywalled. Only the BofA Institute PDF, the Citi GPS page, the IFR releases, the S&P release, the Stanford Canaries paper and Stanford HAI, BLS, and the stockanalysis data pages were read as primary sources.
3. **Unit definitions.** Shipments, production, units in operation and "market" (OEM revenue vs. TAM including services) differ by source. They are not directly comparable.
4. **Copper data.** ICSG (2026–27 surplus) disagrees with S&P/IEA/JPM (deficits from about 2030). Price forecasts beyond 2027 were not found in readable primary sources.
5. **The copper-per-humanoid estimate** is my engineering estimate, cross-checked against CRU, MS and GGII. No teardown with a measured copper mass was found.
6. **Valuations.** Chinese humanoid equities show bubble characteristics (Leaderdrive ≈75× sales, Unitree ≈88× sales, Unitree −59% from its debut open).
7. **Unchecked FX.** EUR/USD 1.17 was assumed, not looked up.
